# Repurposing Guide

**Purpose.** Method for getting more out of every video already made: assigning content to a pillar, deciding refresh vs publish, building playlists that hold viewers, adapting one asset to eight platforms without copy-pasting, extracting Shorts that stand on their own, and translating into other language markets.

**Numbers live in `benchmarks.md`** — Shorts specs and freshness window (§8), video length bands (§3), cadence effects (§4), SEO limits (§6). This file never restates them.

**Contents:** 1 Hub/Hero/Help · 2 Evergreen vs trending · 3 Refresh vs publish · 4 Playlists and series · 5 Platform adaptation specs · 6 Long-form → Shorts · 7 Shorts → long-form · 8 Publication schedule · 9 Asset naming and tracking · 10 Translation and dubbing · 11 Effort/return ranking · 12 Rights and watermarks · 13 Decision rules

## 1. Hub / Hero / Help

| Pillar | Cadence | Job | Typical formats | Success metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hero** | 2–3 per year | Mass awareness; a reason for a stranger to notice the channel | Collaboration, big production, launch, event, milestone | New-viewer reach, subscriber spike |
| **Hub** | The regular rhythm | Turn viewers into a returning audience | Series, weekly show, podcast, behind-the-scenes | Returning-viewer share |
| **Help** | Always-on, published whenever a gap appears | Capture search intent forever | Tutorial, how-to, FAQ, comparison, troubleshooting | Search traffic share, longevity |

**How to assign a video to a pillar** — ask in this order:

1. *Would someone type this into a search box?* → **Help.**
2. *Does it only make sense to someone who already watches?* (part 4 of a series, a channel-update, an inside joke) → **Hub.**
3. *Is it a once-a-year swing that would justify telling people about the channel?* → **Hero.**
4. *None of the above* → it is probably an unfocused Hub video. Sharpen it into one of the three or cut it.

**Balance rule.** Most channels should be majority Hub + Help. A channel that is all Help grows in views but never in audience — nobody subscribes to a search result. A channel that is all Hub grows an audience it can never expand, because there is no discovery surface. A channel that is all Hero exhausts the creator. If a quarter has no Help video, discovery will stall; if it has no Hub video, retention of existing subscribers will.

## 2. Evergreen vs trending

A planning heuristic, not a benchmark — `benchmarks.md` holds no measured figure for this split.

| Type | Target share | Decay profile | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evergreen | The clear majority of slots | Slow; can earn for years | Compounding library, search, affiliate, ad revenue |
| Trending | The minority, deliberately capped | Fast; most views inside days | Reach spikes, relevance, algorithmic momentum |

Trending content is not wasted — it buys attention that evergreen content then converts. But a channel built on trending has to keep sprinting forever, because the library is worth nothing next quarter. Judge the mix quarterly, not per video.

**Trending has a decision deadline.** If a topic will be stale before the video can ship at the channel's normal production speed, do not make it. A late trending video underperforms an ordinary evergreen video *and* costs the slot.

## 3. Refresh vs publish

An old video that still ranks is a renewable asset. Deciding between refreshing it and publishing something new:

| Signal | Refresh the old video | Publish a new one |
|---|---|---|
| The old video still gets impressions | Yes | — |
| Its information is wrong or dated, but the topic is not | Yes | — |
| The topic changed fundamentally | — | Yes (and link from the old one) |
| Retention is fine but CTR has decayed | Yes — repackage only | — |
| CTR fine, retention poor | — | Yes; the video itself is the problem |
| Old video has strong backlinks or embeds | Yes — never delete it | — |
| The angle is genuinely different | — | Yes |

**Refresh ladder — cheapest first, stop when the metric recovers:**
1. **Repackage.** New thumbnail, new title. No re-edit. Hours of work, sometimes doubles a video's impressions.
2. **Metadata refresh.** Description, chapters, tags, pinned comment, end-screen targets.
3. **Partial re-edit.** Replace outdated segments, add an on-screen "updated" card, re-upload as an update only if the changes are substantial.
4. **Full remake.** New video on the same keyword; keep the old one live and link forward. Never delete a ranking video to replace it — you lose its history.

**Never** delete and re-upload just to reset the publish date. It destroys accumulated watch time, backlinks and comments, and the algorithm does not reward the reset.

## 4. Playlists and series as retention instruments

A playlist is not organization; it is a session-length instrument. It ranks independently in search and it changes what plays next.

| Design decision | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Roughly 5–10 videos | Long enough to extend a session, short enough to feel finishable |
| First video | The strongest performer, not the oldest | The first video decides whether the playlist gets a chance at all |
| Order | Logical for the viewer, not chronological for the creator | "Beginner → advanced" beats "January → December" |
| Naming | Keyword-first, benefit second, no channel branding | Playlist titles are search assets |
| Description | Written, not blank; primary keyword early | It is indexed |
| Series playlists | Use for anything with a required watch order | Enforces sequence, signals connected content |
| Count | Few and curated | Twenty half-empty playlists is worse than five full ones |
| Maintenance | Add new videos to existing playlists on publish | The most-skipped 30-second task in the whole workflow |

**Series strategy.** A series is a promise to return. Keep cycles short (roughly 5–7 episodes) so the creator can finish what they started. Number episodes in the title, keep a consistent visual signature in the thumbnail, end each episode by naming what the next one covers, and put the whole run in a series playlist. An unfinished series is worse than no series: it teaches the audience that the channel's promises expire.

## 5. Per-platform adaptation specs

The rule: **each platform gets a native adaptation, never a copy-paste.** What always stays the same is the *insight*. What always changes is the *packaging*.

| Platform | Format constraint | Tone shift | Hook adaptation | Change | Keep |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TikTok** | Vertical 9:16, short; native captions; sound-on culture | Casual, fast, unpolished is an advantage | First 1s must be motion or a claim; no logo intro | Re-shoot the hook natively; add on-screen text; use trending audio only if it fits | The single idea; the payoff |
| **Instagram Reels** | Vertical 9:16, short; strong aesthetic bias | Polished, visually composed | Visual hook before verbal hook; safe zones for UI overlay | Tighter framing; cover image chosen deliberately; caption written for saves | The idea; the visual quality |
| **LinkedIn** | Native video or text post; autoplay muted | Professional, first-person, lesson-shaped | Text hook carries it, not the video; open with the conclusion | Reframe the topic as a professional lesson; open with the result, then the story | The substance; the specificity |
| **X** | Thread or short native clip; text-dominant | Terse, opinionated, argumentative | The first post must stand alone as a claim | Break into a hook post + 4–6 insight posts + a link post; one idea per post | The argument's logic |
| **Facebook** | Square or vertical; older, sound-off default | Warm, plainspoken, community-oriented | Burned-in captions are mandatory | Longer setup is tolerated; explicit framing for groups | The story |
| **Blog post** | 800–2,000 words; embed the video | Structured, scannable, reference-shaped | Headline is the SEO asset; H2s carry the structure | Add what the video could not show: tables, links, code, citations | The argument and every claim |
| **Email newsletter** | One idea; a subject line and preview text | Personal, direct, one-to-one | Subject line is the entire hook | Distil to ~200 words plus a link; write one clear reason to click | The single takeaway |
| **Podcast feed** | Audio only, full length | Conversational | Audio-first cold open; a spoken intro replacing on-screen context | Strip visual references, re-record a short intro explaining what would have been shown | The full conversation |

**Universal adaptation checks.** Does it make sense to someone who has never seen the channel? Does it work with the sound off? Does it stand alone if the link is never clicked? A repurposed asset that only makes sense as an advertisement for the original is a bad asset — each one should be worth consuming on its own, and the click is a bonus.

**Podcast caveat:** only interview, discussion and monologue formats survive the strip to audio. A tutorial that depends on showing a screen becomes unlistenable. Test by closing your eyes and playing the video; if you are lost within a minute, it is not a podcast episode.

## 6. Long-form → Shorts extraction

Specs, length bands, hook window and freshness: `benchmarks.md` §8.

**Step 1 — find the beats that stand alone.** Scan the transcript for moments matching any of these, and mark timestamps:

| Beat type | Signal in the transcript |
|---|---|
| The surprising claim | "Most people think… but actually…" |
| The single number | A statistic that lands without setup |
| The demonstration | A visible before/after |
| The mistake | "The thing I got wrong was…" |
| The one-step tip | A complete, useful action in under 30 seconds |
| The strong opinion | A sentence someone would argue with |
| The emotional peak | The reaction, the reveal, the laugh |

**Step 2 — test standalone-ness.** Play the candidate to someone who has not seen the video. If they need context you did not give, either the clip needs a re-hook (step 3) or it is not a Short.

**Step 3 — re-hook the clip.** A clip that had context in the long-form has none now. Three ways to restore it, in order of preference:
1. **Record a new opening line** (3–5 seconds) that supplies the missing setup and states the payoff.
2. **On-screen text** that frames the clip before the audio starts making sense.
3. **Re-cut the entry point** to start on the strongest sentence, not on the sentence before it.
The most common extraction failure is starting the clip where the segment started in the long-form. Almost always, the clip should start 5–15 seconds later.

**Step 4 — end it deliberately.** Options: (a) end on the payoff and let it loop — best for the loop metric; (b) end on an open question that drives comments; (c) end with a specific pointer to the long-form ("the full breakdown is on the channel"), used sparingly because the conversion rate is low. Never end on a fade or on the creator trailing off — the loop should be seamless.

**Volume.** A 20–30 minute video usually yields 5–15 viable clips, of which perhaps half are worth finishing. Extract more than you publish and keep the surplus as a buffer for weeks when production slips.

## 7. Shorts → long-form (the reverse direction)

Under-used and cheaper than it looks. Shorts are a *test bed*: a Short is a cheap experiment on a hook, a topic and a thumbnail concept.

| Signal from a Short | What it justifies |
|---|---|
| Unusually high completion vs the channel's norm | The hook works — build a long-form around that exact opening |
| Comment section full of "how do I…" | The long-form is the answer to the top question |
| Saves and shares far above norm | The topic has reference value — make the definitive version |
| Strong performance on a topic outside the usual pillars | A test of expansion, before betting a full production on it |

**Assembly patterns:** compile 5–8 related Shorts into a themed long-form with new connective narration and a proper intro (works when the Shorts are genuinely related, fails as a lazy stitch); expand one Short into a full treatment with the Short's hook as the long-form's opening; or turn a Short's comment thread into a Q&A video.

## 8. Publication schedule

Relative to the main upload at **day 0**. Adapt to capacity — the shape matters more than the exact days.

| When | Action | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Day −3 to −1 | Community post teaser or poll on the topic | Warms the audience; the poll doubles as validation (`community-guide.md`) |
| Day 0 | Long-form publishes; add to playlists; pin a comment that seeds discussion | The core asset |
| Day 0 | Community post announcing it, with the thumbnail | The one post with reliable reach for a new upload |
| Day 0–1 | Email newsletter | Highest-intent audience, sent while it is new |
| Day 1 | Short #1 — the strongest clip | Reach spike while the long-form is still fresh |
| Day 1–2 | X thread or LinkedIn post | Different audience, no cannibalization |
| Day 2–4 | Short #2 | Sustains the second wave |
| Day 3–5 | Blog post with the video embedded | Search asset; slow-burn traffic |
| Day 5–7 | Short #3; Instagram/TikTok versions | Platform-native audiences |
| Day 7+ | Podcast episode if the format allows | Audio audience, no urgency |
| Day 14–30 | Short #4 from an unused clip | Extends the life of the production |
| Day 30+ | Retire clips from the Shorts pipeline (see §8 freshness window) | Old clips no longer earn their slot |

**Why staggered rather than same-day everywhere:** posting everything at once competes with yourself for the same audience's attention on the same afternoon, and it collapses a week of surface area into one day. Staggering also lets a strong signal from one platform inform what you post next.

## 9. Asset naming and tracking

The failure this prevents: three months in, nobody can find which clip came from which video, which platform got which cut, or whether clip #7 was ever posted.

**Naming convention.** `YYYY-MM-DD_video-slug_asset-type_nn_lang`
Examples: `2026-03-14_battery-myths_short_03_en` · `2026-03-14_battery-myths_thread_01_en` · `2026-03-14_battery-myths_reel_02_pt`

Rules: ASCII only, kebab-case, no spaces, date first so files sort chronologically, the video slug matching the workspace folder so every asset traces back to its source, a two-digit sequence so ordering survives, and a language tag from the start even on a monolingual channel — retrofitting it later is painful.

**Tracking table** — one per video, kept in the video's workspace folder:

| Asset ID | Type | Source timestamp | Platform | Status | Posted | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `..._short_01_en` | Short | 04:12–04:47 | YouTube Shorts | Posted | 2026-03-15 | Note the outcome |
| `..._short_02_en` | Short | 11:03–11:38 | TikTok, Reels | Rendered | — | — |
| `..._thread_01_en` | X thread | whole video | X | Drafted | — | — |

Status vocabulary: `identified` → `rendered` → `drafted` → `scheduled` → `posted` → `retired`. Anything sitting in `identified` for a month should be dropped rather than carried.

## 10. Translation, dubbing and multi-language repurposing

The largest untapped lever for most channels, because the production cost is already sunk. Full treatment of market selection, RPM effects, keyword differences and one-channel-vs-many: `localization-guide.md`.

| Approach | Effort | What it unlocks | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auto-generated captions | None | Baseline accessibility; some search value | Errors on names and jargon — always review |
| Human-corrected captions in the original language | Low | Accuracy, better indexing, better auto-translation downstream | — |
| Translated subtitle tracks | Low–medium | Reach into markets that read subtitles | Machine translation of idiom and humour reads badly |
| Translated title and description per language | Low | Discovery in that language — often the highest-return single step | Keywords do not translate directly (`localization-guide.md`) |
| Multi-language audio tracks | Medium–high | Native-language listening on the same video, keeping one video's accumulated authority | Quality of the dub is the whole ballgame |
| Fully localized separate uploads | High | Full control per market | Fragments the catalogue and the audience |

**Sequence:** correct captions in the original language first (everything downstream inherits their errors), then translate titles and descriptions for the one or two markets already visible in Analytics, then subtitles for those markets, then audio tracks only if the data justifies it. **Do not** localize into a market with no existing audience signal on a hunch; localize where the audience already is.

## 11. Effort / return ranking

For a solo creator who cannot do everything. Return is *relative to the effort*, not absolute.

| Rank | Workflow | Effort | Return | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add the video to the right playlists | Minutes | High | Never skip |
| 2 | Community post on publish day | Minutes | High | Never skip |
| 3 | Pinned comment seeding discussion | Minutes | High | Never skip |
| 4 | 2–3 Shorts from the strongest beats | Hours | High | Core workflow |
| 5 | Email newsletter | ~30 min | High if a list exists | Do it if the list exists |
| 6 | Translated title + description for one market | ~30 min | High if the audience is there | Check Analytics first |
| 7 | Repackaging an old video | Hours | High, and repeatable | Do this before making a new video |
| 8 | X thread / LinkedIn post | ~30 min | Medium; audience-dependent | Only where the creator already has a presence |
| 9 | TikTok / Reels cross-post | Low (assets exist) | Medium | Cheap once Shorts exist |
| 10 | Blog post | Hours | Medium, slow-burn | Only if the site exists and is maintained |
| 11 | Podcast feed | Medium, recurring | Low unless the format fits | Skip for visual formats |
| 12 | Full dubbing | High | High but only in a proven market | Last, and only on evidence |

**Rule:** ranks 1–4 are the floor. A creator doing only those out-performs one doing all twelve badly.

## 12. Rights and watermarks

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| No cross-platform watermarks | Exporting from one short-video app and posting to another carries a watermark that the receiving platform suppresses. Always export a clean master and re-upload. |
| Export clean masters | Keep an un-branded, un-captioned master of every clip so each platform's version is built from source. |
| Music licensing does not travel | A track cleared for one platform's library is not cleared elsewhere. Re-check per platform; an unlicensed track can mute, block or demonetize the post. |
| Music affects Shorts revenue | Licensed tracks split the revenue pool (`benchmarks.md` §8) — a decision, not a detail. |
| Third-party footage | Fair-use-style reasoning does not port across platforms or jurisdictions. Clips reused off-platform carry more risk, not less. |
| Guest and collaborator consent | Get consent for cross-platform reuse at recording time, not at posting time. |
| Sponsored segments | Never repurpose a sponsored segment to a platform the contract did not cover — that is unlicensed usage (`monetization-guide.md` §5). |
| Disclosure travels with the content | A repurposed clip containing a paid endorsement needs its own disclosure on the new platform (`monetization-guide.md` §11). |

## 13. Decision rules

- **If** a video does not clearly fit Hero, Hub or Help → sharpen it until it does, or cut it.
- **If** a quarter has no Help video → discovery will stall. **If** it has no Hub video → returning viewers will decay.
- **If** a trending topic will be stale before the channel can ship at its normal speed → do not make it.
- **If** an old video still gets impressions but CTR has decayed → repackage it before making anything new. That is the highest return per hour available.
- **If** a video is being replaced → keep the old one live and link forward. Never delete a ranking video.
- **If** creating a playlist → strongest video first, keyword-first name, written description, 5–10 videos, added to on every publish.
- **If** starting a series → commit to 5–7 episodes, number them, and finish. An abandoned series costs more trust than it earned.
- **If** adapting to a platform → change the packaging, keep the insight. If the asset only makes sense as an ad for the original, rebuild it.
- **If** extracting a Short → verify it stands alone for someone who has not seen the source; if it does not, re-hook it or drop it. Start the clip later than the segment did.
- **If** a Short over-performs → it is a validated hook. Build the long-form on it.
- **If** repurposing → stagger over the days after publication; never post everything on day 0.
- **If** the creator can only do four things → playlists, community post, pinned comment, two Shorts. In that order.
- **If** localizing → follow the audience that already exists in Analytics; correct the original captions first; never localize on a hunch.
- **If** cross-posting → clean master, no foreign watermark, re-verify music rights, re-apply any disclosure.
