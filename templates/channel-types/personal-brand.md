# Personal Brand Channel

> The person is the format. Viewers subscribe to a point of view, then buy from it off-platform.

---

## 1. Channel DNA

A personal-brand channel sells **continuity of a person**, not the resolution of a query, which
is why it wins on browse and external rather than search: the home feed serves it to people who
already watched once, and off-platform surfaces (newsletter, podcast, social) push traffic in at
a low 2.8% CTR but very high intent (`benchmarks.md` §1). This is the **only archetype that
monetizes before YouTube monetizes it** — product-funnel revenue requires no YPP threshold at
all, so a 400-subscriber channel with an email list can outearn a 40,000-subscriber channel
without one. The practical consequence is that the **email list, not the subscriber count, is
the asset**: subscribers are rented from an algorithm, list members are owned. Because the
channel is a person rather than a subject, positioning has to be a **one-sentence filter** —
who this is for and what changes for them — or the content sprawls into an unrecommendable mix
and the algorithm cannot learn who to serve it to. AdSense here is mid-tier at best and swings
enormously with subject matter (`benchmarks.md` §9), which is exactly why it should never be the
plan. The characteristic trap is **perfectionism against a format that rewards volume**: the
creator polishes for three weeks, publishes four videos a quarter, and never reaches the 12+
uploads/month band associated with 8× faster view growth (`benchmarks.md` §4) — while the
audience, who came for the person, would happily have watched all twelve rough ones.

## 2. Content Mix

| Type | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Hub** (recurring signature formats) | 45–55% | The habit. This is what "watching you" means in practice. |
| **Help** (searchable, problem-shaped) | 25–35% | The only cold-discovery surface this archetype has. |
| **Hero** (ambitious, shareable, positioning-defining) | 15–20% | Reach beyond the existing audience; 3–4 per year. |

**Evergreen vs trending:** 50–60% evergreen. Higher than a vlog, lower than education —
personality content dates, but the positioning pieces should still be watchable in two years.

**Series beat one-offs.** A named recurring format gives browse a repeatable pattern to
recognize and gives the viewer a reason to expect the next one.

## 3. Cadence & Length

| Setup | Long-form | Shorts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 2/week (8–10/month) | 3–5/week | Volume over polish; batch-record in one session |
| Small team (2–3) | 3/week (12+/month) | 5–7/week | Clears the 12+/month growth threshold (`benchmarks.md` §4) |

**Optimal length:** 8–12 minutes, the entertainment/vlog band (`benchmarks.md` §3). Longer only
when the material is a genuine deep-dive; this audience tolerates length for a person they like
but the retention curve does not.

**Mid-roll:** clear 8:00 to unlock mid-rolls (~50% revenue increase, `benchmarks.md` §3). Do not
pad to reach it — for this archetype the funnel, not the ad break, is the revenue.

**Running both formats matters here more than most:** long-form plus Shorts together grows
40–60% faster than either alone (`benchmarks.md` §4), and Shorts are the cheapest way to put a
face in front of a cold audience.

## 4. Title Patterns

1. `Why I [UNEXPECTED DECISION]`
2. `The [NUMBER] Rules I Live By as a [ROLE]`
3. `I Tried [APPROACH] for [TIME PERIOD]`
4. `What [MILESTONE] Actually Taught Me`
5. `Stop [COMMON BEHAVIOR]. Do This Instead.`
6. `My Honest Take on [CONTESTED TOPIC]`
7. `How I [ACHIEVED OUTCOME] Without [EXPECTED REQUIREMENT]`
8. `[NUMBER] Things I'd Tell My [EARLIER-STAGE SELF]`
9. `The Truth About [INDUSTRY / LIFESTYLE]`
10. `A Day of [SPECIFIC WORK] — Unedited`

**Length rule:** browse-dominant archetype, so keep titles **under 50 characters** — they have
to read at a glance in a scrolling feed. The hook and any keyword must sit in the first 40–50
characters (mobile truncation, `benchmarks.md` §6). Numbers add +20–30% CTR (`benchmarks.md` §6),
which is why four of the ten formulas carry one. **Non-English channel:** the formulas are English sentence shapes — keep the structure and re-derive the wording from how that audience actually phrases things, because a translated pattern reads as imported (`references/localization-guide.md` §5).

## 5. Thumbnail Formula

- **Face:** mandatory, and it must be *your* face — this is the one archetype where facial
  recognition is the packaging. Faces carry +20–30% CTR (`benchmarks.md` §7); here the effect
  compounds because returning viewers recognize the person before they read anything.
- **Text:** ≤5 words, 3 ideal (`benchmarks.md` §7). The text carries the emotional hook; the title
  carries the promise — never the same words (Information Split Rule).
- **Colors:** 2–3, held constant across the channel. Consistency is the substitute for a
  brand logo.
- **Composition:** exactly one focal point, 30–40% negative space, face occupying roughly a
  third of the frame. Expression should be legible at thumbnail size in under one second.
- **Avoid:** rotating visual styles, stock backgrounds, shocked-face templates that contradict
  a considered positioning, and any thumbnail that would work equally well on someone else's
  channel.
- **Target CTR:** 3.5% on browse traffic, 5%+ blended is healthy (`benchmarks.md` §1). Use
  Test & Compare (up to 3 variants, ~2 weeks, `benchmarks.md` §7) on hero videos.

## 6. Hook Style

**Primary — Story Open.** Drop the viewer into a specific moment with a consequence attached.
The person is the product, so a scene does more positioning work than a thesis.
> "Two weeks after I signed the lease, the biggest client I had left without a phone call."

**Secondary — Direct Challenge.** Address the viewer's current behavior head-on and make the
video the alternative.
> "If you're still doing this the way I did for three years, this video is going to be
> uncomfortable."

**Timing:** state the value proposition within 15 seconds (+18% retention at the one-minute
mark, `benchmarks.md` §2). 55% of viewers are lost in the first 60 seconds and 20% in the
first 10 — a pattern interrupt inside the first 5 seconds is worth +23% retention
(`benchmarks.md` §2).

Full taxonomy in `references/hook-library.md`.

## 7. Monetization Stack

> All figures are **US baseline**. Apply `references/localization-guide.md` before quoting
> revenue for a non-US channel.

| Rank | Stream | Why This Position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | External funnels | Requires no YPP threshold at all — this archetype earns before it is monetized. Course, cohort, service or book converts against personal trust |
| 2 | Brand deals | Sponsors buy the person's endorsement, not the view count; floor $1,000+ and 5K–15K average views already commands $1,000–1,500/video (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 3 | Memberships | A parasocial relationship is exactly what a membership sells; 1% conversion is meaningful, start with 2–3 tiers (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 4 | AdSense | Real but secondary and subject-dependent — Lifestyle RPM $3–6 versus Business $15–30 (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 5 | Shopping affiliate | Works when the person's setup, tools or wardrobe is itself content; 5–20% commission (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 6 | Super Chat / Thanks | Only meaningful if the channel streams; otherwise a tip jar, $50–500/mo at mid size (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 7 | Shorts ad share | Reach instrument, not revenue — long-form RPM runs 10–100× Shorts RPM (`benchmarks.md` §9) |

**The stack inverts the usual order deliberately.** Ranks 1–3 all pay before 1,000 subscribers
if the offer exists. A personal-brand channel with no capture mechanism has thrown away its
best two streams and left itself competing on the RPM of whatever subject it happens to cover.

## 8. Growth Trajectory

| Tier | What Changes | Key Lever | Revenue Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–500 | Positioning, not reach, is the constraint — the audience has to be able to say in one sentence what this person is for | Fix positioning to one sentence; publish consistently; open the email capture on day one | Usually none from the platform, though an offer can sell to a list of any size |
| 500–1K | Expanded YPP at 500 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9) opens memberships and Super Thanks, but the list is what actually changes the picture | A first paid offer to the list, before platform monetization is even available | Off-platform first; anything the platform pays is immaterial at this size |
| 1K–10K | Full YPP at 1,000 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9) adds ads, which stay a minor line — the list becomes the primary revenue channel | A named recurring format, so the audience has a reason to return on a schedule | Meaningful but not replacement income, and set by offer price rather than by views |
| 10K–50K | Sponsorships arrive inbound and the offer can be productized, which decouples income from hours worked | Productize the offer instead of trading hours | Can support full-time focus, with revenue far less predictable than in any ad-primary archetype |
| 50K–100K+ | The channel becomes top-of-funnel for a business, with team leverage behind production | Team leverage on production; the channel as acquisition for the business | Replacement income and beyond, set by offer economics rather than by any RPM band |

Revenue here is far less predictable than in ad-primary archetypes because it depends on offer price and list conversion
rather than on views — a small channel with a high-priced offer can outearn a very large channel monetizing on ads
alone, so audience size is a poor proxy for income in this archetype.

> ⚠️ Tier progression is not a timeline. `benchmarks.md` §11 lists growth-timeline and revenue-by-tier as known gaps — no verified data exists. Any revenue figure must be modeled from the creator's own audience size and geography via `references/localization-guide.md`, never read off a table.

## 9. Failure Modes

**Perfectionism against a volume format.** Each video is rewritten, reshot and recut until it is
"ready", producing four uploads a quarter. The channel never approaches 12+ uploads/month, so it
never gets the 8× view growth and 3× subscriber growth associated with that cadence
(`benchmarks.md` §4) — and the audience, who came for the person, would have watched the rough
cut.
**Fix:** set a hard production ceiling of 6 hours per video and publish whatever exists at hour
6. Batch-record four videos in one session so the marginal cost of shipping drops below the cost
of deliberating.

**Positioning sprawl.** Without a one-sentence filter the channel drifts across unrelated topics,
the algorithm cannot identify a viewer profile to serve it to, browse impressions flatten, and
traffic concentrates on whatever single video went wide.
**Fix:** write the filter as "I help [SPECIFIC PERSON] do [SPECIFIC THING]" and kill every idea
on the next 10-video slate that fails it. Put the sentence in the channel banner and the
description's first 150 characters (`benchmarks.md` §6).

**Renting the audience with nowhere to send it.** Subscribers accumulate, AdSense pays lifestyle
RPM of $3–6 (`benchmarks.md` §9), and the creator concludes the channel doesn't work — when in
fact the two highest-ranked revenue streams for this archetype were never built.
**Fix:** ship one capture mechanism this week (a single-page opt-in with one genuinely useful
resource), pin it in every description's first two lines, and mention it verbally once per video
at a natural point — not at the end, where 55% of the audience is already gone.
