# Vlog Channel

> Subscriptions-driven. The viewer did not come for the topic. They came for the person, and
> they will keep coming until they don't.

---

## 1. Channel DNA

In a vlog the **relationship is the product**, which changes what every standard metric means.
Views arrive through the subscriptions feed and the notification bell rather than through
discovery, so CTR peaks in the first 24 hours against a warm audience at 12%+ and settles into
the 4–8% sustained band (`benchmarks.md` §1, §10) — the launch spike here is a measure of
attachment, not of packaging. That makes **subscriber quality beat subscriber count and churn the
metric that matters**: 100,000 subscribers who no longer watch produce less revenue and less
algorithmic momentum than 20,000 who open every upload, and the ratio of views to subscribers is
the honest health reading. The structural weakness is discovery. A browse or search viewer is
looking for a topic; this archetype's appeal is a person they have not met, so cold traffic
converts poorly and the channel cannot grow by optimizing keywords the way a tutorial channel
can — growth comes from collaborations, from Shorts, and from individual videos that happen to
have a topic strong enough to travel without the relationship. Money follows the relationship
rather than the RPM: Lifestyle sits at $3–6 (`benchmarks.md` §9), so **brand deals outrank
AdSense**, and they pay disproportionately well because a host recommendation inside a
relationship converts in a way a pre-roll never will. The characteristic trap is confusing
**footage with story** — a week of clips assembled chronologically has no tension, no question
and no resolution, retention decays steadily from the first minute, and the creator concludes
their life is not interesting enough when the actual problem is that nothing in the video was
ever at stake.

## 2. Content Mix

| Type | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Hub** (the recurring life-in-progress format) | 55–60% | The relationship engine. Consistency here is what churn responds to. |
| **Hero** (milestone, journey, or high-concept episode) | 25–30% | The only reliable source of new audience — a topic strong enough to travel beyond subscribers. |
| **Help** (setup, routine, how-I-do-X videos) | 15% | The archetype's only searchable, compounding layer. Punches far above its share in discovery. |

**Evergreen vs trending:** 30–40% evergreen, concentrated almost entirely in the Help tier. Daily
life content decays fast; the "how I actually do [thing]" video is what a stranger finds two years
later.

**Series beat episodes.** A multi-part arc with an unresolved question gives subscribers a reason
to return on a schedule and gives the algorithm session continuation — the third-ranked
satisfaction signal (`benchmarks.md` §5).

## 3. Cadence & Length

| Setup | Long-form | Shorts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 1–2/week | 3–5/week | Predictability matters more than volume — the same day each week trains the return habit |
| Small team (2–3) | 2–3/week | 5/week | Long-form plus Shorts together grows 40–60% faster than either alone (`benchmarks.md` §4) |

**Optimal length:** 8–12 minutes (`benchmarks.md` §3). Vlogs pass 12 minutes only when a genuine
arc requires it; the 5–10 minute band holds the highest average retention on the platform at
31.5%, and unearned runtime here reads as self-indulgence rather than depth.

**Mid-roll:** 8:00 unlocks mid-rolls for roughly a 50% revenue increase (`benchmarks.md` §3),
which is meaningful against a $3–6 RPM. Place the break at a scene transition.

**A publishing break is more expensive here than anywhere else.** It costs 2–3 weeks of rebuilt
momentum (`benchmarks.md` §4), and in a relationship-driven archetype it also breaks the habit
that the subscriptions feed depends on. Respect the notification cap of 3 per user per 24 hours
(`benchmarks.md` §4) — bunched uploads suppress each other.

## 4. Title Patterns

1. `The Day [UNEXPECTED EVENT] Happened`
2. `[TIME PERIOD] of [PURSUIT] — What Actually Changed`
3. `I Moved to [PLACE]. Here's the Part Nobody Posts.`
4. `We [MAJOR LIFE ACTION] and It [OUTCOME]`
5. `A Realistic Day [DOING ACTIVITY] in [PLACE]`
6. `[N] Months Into [BIG CHANGE]: An Honest Update`
7. `Everything Went Wrong in [PLACE OR SITUATION]`
8. `What [MILESTONE] Actually Costs`
9. `The [ADJECTIVE] Reason I'm [DECISION]`
10. `[ACTIVITY] Alone for [DURATION]`

**Length rule:** subscriptions-dominant archetype, so titles are read by people who already know
the creator — keep them under 50 characters and spend the space on the *situation*, not on
identifying the channel (`benchmarks.md` §6). Never put the creator's own name in the title: it is
the one piece of information the entire audience already has. For a Hero or Help video aimed at
strangers, switch to the search rules — 60–70 characters with the topic front-loaded in the first
40–50. **Non-English channel:** keep the structures and replace the language — re-derive the phrasing from how that audience actually writes and searches, never by translating the English patterns (`references/localization-guide.md` §5).

## 5. Thumbnail Formula

- **Face:** essential, and it should be the creator's — recognition is the click. Faces lift CTR
  20–30% (`benchmarks.md` §7), and here the face carries the relationship rather than an emotion.
  Show genuine expression in a real environment; posed studio shots break the format's premise.
- **Text:** ≤5 words, 3 ideal (`benchmarks.md` §7). This archetype *prefers* the short end — often no
  text at all. When present it names the situation, not the feeling — `DAY 1`, `IT FLOODED`,
  `LAST DAY`.
- **Colors:** 2–3, warmer and less saturated than an entertainment channel would use. Consistency
  across the channel is what makes the thumbnail recognizable at a glance in the subscriptions
  feed.
- **Composition:** exactly one focal point with 30–40% negative space (`benchmarks.md` §7). Face
  plus environment is the archetype's core structure — the environment tells the subscriber what
  this episode is, and the face tells them it is this channel.
- **Avoid:** exaggerated shock faces (they contradict the authenticity the audience subscribed
  for), heavy graphic overlays, stock-looking compositions, and thumbnails where the person is
  too small to recognize at mobile size — 70%+ of views are mobile.
- **Target CTR:** 12%+ in the first 24 hours to the warm audience, 4–8% sustained
  (`benchmarks.md` §10). A falling launch-window CTR against stable subscriber count is the
  earliest available churn signal — read it before the view count reflects it.

## 6. Hook Style

**Primary — Story Open.** Start inside the moment of highest tension in the episode, then step
back. This is the single device that separates a vlog from footage.
> "This is twenty minutes before we found out the visa had been refused. We had already given up
> the flat."

**Secondary — Stakes Framing.** Name what is being risked or decided in this episode, so there is
a question the video answers.
> "By tonight we either have somewhere to live or we're driving back. That's the whole day."

**Timing:** state the value proposition within 15 seconds — the +18% retention effect at the
1-minute mark (`benchmarks.md` §2) applies here as much as anywhere, and "value" in a vlog is the
question the episode will resolve. Never open with a greeting, a channel intro, or a recap of the
last video; 20% of viewers are gone in the first 10 seconds (`benchmarks.md` §2). Place a pattern
interrupt roughly every 30 seconds, and close every episode on an open loop pointing at the next
one — the suspension-bridge curve correlates with 68% higher completion (`benchmarks.md` §2).

Full taxonomy in `references/hook-library.md`.

## 7. Monetization Stack

> All figures are **US baseline**. Apply `references/localization-guide.md` before quoting
> revenue for a non-US channel.

| Rank | Stream | Why This Position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brand deals | A host read inside a relationship is the highest-converting ad format on the platform; 5K–15K average views already pays $1,000–1,500 per video (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 2 | Memberships | The relationship is the product, so selling more of it is the natural second stream; 1% conversion is meaningful and 2–3 tiers is the right start (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 3 | AdSense | Lifestyle RPM is only $3–6 (`benchmarks.md` §9); dependable, but it will never be the main line |
| 4 | External funnels | Patreon, a newsletter, or merchandise — an audience attached to a person follows them off-platform better than any other archetype's |
| 5 | Shopping affiliate | Genuine when the vlog naturally shows gear, clothing or products in use; 5–20% commission, 30-day attribution (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 6 | Super Chat / Thanks | Real but small unless the channel streams; $50–500/mo for mid-size channels (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 7 | Shorts ad share | $0.01–0.05 per 1K in comedy/lifestyle (`benchmarks.md` §9) — valuable as the archetype's best discovery instrument, not as income |

## 8. Growth Trajectory

| Tier | What Changes | Key Lever | Revenue Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–500 | Discovery is structurally weak from the start; the tier goes to finding the person's actual on-camera register, not to optimizing packaging | Fixed publishing day; find an on-camera register that is sustainable for years | None — not yet monetized |
| 500–1K | Expanded YPP at 500 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9) opens memberships and Super Thanks — this audience supports a person, so audience-direct arrives before ads | Memberships and Super Thanks turned on at unlock, not deferred until ads exist | Audience-direct only, and immaterial at this size |
| 1K–10K | Full YPP at 1,000 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9); the discovery weakness becomes the binding constraint, and collaborations and Shorts are the only reliable fixes | Collaborations and Shorts to solve the discovery weakness the format creates | First ad revenue at the Tier 3 RPM band (Lifestyle, `benchmarks.md` §9); small relative to audience size |
| 10K–50K | Brand deals reach the floor (`benchmarks.md` §9) and become the main line — what a sponsor buys here is the audience's trust, not its size | Series arcs to hold churn down; a rate card priced on trust rather than on reach | Meaningful but not replacement income from ads; sponsorship is what makes it material |
| 50K–100K+ | The audience is attached to a person and follows off platform, which makes income durable through format changes | Sponsorship rate card plus membership base and an off-platform funnel | Replacement-income territory, driven by sponsorship and audience-direct rather than by the Tier 3 RPM band |

Slower to start than browse-driven archetypes because discovery is structurally weak, and more durable once established
because the audience is attached to a person rather than to a topic — which also means revenue rises with audience trust
rather than in step with audience size.

> ⚠️ Tier progression is not a timeline. `benchmarks.md` §11 lists growth-timeline and revenue-by-tier as known gaps — no verified data exists. Any revenue figure must be modeled from the creator's own audience size and geography via `references/localization-guide.md`, never read off a table.

## 9. Failure Modes

**Footage, not story.** The episode is a chronological assembly of the week. There is no question
posed and none answered, retention declines steadily from the first minute, and the creator
concludes their life is not interesting enough.
**Fix:** before editing, write one sentence naming the question the episode answers and one
naming how it resolves. If either cannot be written, the footage is B-roll for a different
episode — do not publish it as one. Cut the timeline so the resolution lands in the final 90
seconds.

**Churn invisible behind subscriber growth.** Subscribers keep rising while views per video stay
flat or fall. The channel reads the subscriber chart, feels fine, and does not notice the
relationship decaying until a sponsor asks about average views.
**Fix:** track views-per-video divided by subscribers as a monthly figure in the channel
dashboard, alongside the 24-hour launch CTR. When the ratio falls three months running, cut
cadence and raise per-episode story quality rather than publishing more.

**Nothing a stranger can find.** 100% of output assumes the viewer already knows the creator, so
there is no video in the catalog that a person with no relationship would ever click. Growth
stalls entirely at whatever the current audience is.
**Fix:** publish one topic-led video per month — a Help or Hero piece built around a subject
someone would search for, titled by the search rules — and end-screen it into the Hub series.
Aim for 6 such videos in the catalog before diagnosing a growth problem any further.
