# Review Channel

> Search plus Google Shopping surfaces. The viewer is holding a credit card and looking for
> permission — or for a reason not to.

---

## 1. Channel DNA

A review channel sells **a decision**, not information, and that is why its economics beat almost
every other archetype at the same subscriber count: purchase intent is the most valuable intent
on the internet, so the audience is worth more to advertisers than its size suggests, the
Tech & Reviews niche posts the third-highest CTR on the platform at 7.5% (`benchmarks.md` §1),
and Shopping affiliate commissions of 5–20% (`benchmarks.md` §9) attach revenue directly to the
moment of decision rather than to the impression. The videos also surface off-platform — 25%+ of
Google results include video snippets and 29.5% of AI Overviews cite YouTube
(`benchmarks.md` §6) — so a review earns a second, external traffic stream that no browse-fed
archetype gets. All of that rests on one asset: **credibility is the entire business, and a
channel that never says no is worth nothing**, because a recommendation that is always positive
carries no information and the audience prices it accordingly. Timing is the second lever —
launch windows concentrate an entire category's search volume into a few days, and a review
published in week one against a well-known product can out-earn six months of ordinary uploads —
while **comparison videos consistently outperform single-product reviews**, because "which one"
is the question a buyer actually has and "is this good" is not. The characteristic trap is the
slow sale of that credibility: not one obvious paid rave, but a gradual drift toward covering
only what vendors send, which quietly converts an audience of buyers into an audience of nobody.

## 2. Content Mix

| Type | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Help** (comparisons, buying guides, "best X for Y") | 55–60% | The compounding base and the strongest affiliate converter. Answers "which one." |
| **Hub** (recurring formats: long-term tests, monthly roundups) | 25–30% | Builds the trust relationship that makes a "don't buy this" believable. |
| **Hero** (launch-window flagship review) | 15% | The reach spike. Timed to the category's release calendar, not to the content calendar. |

**Evergreen vs trending:** 60–70% evergreen. Lower than education or tutorial by necessity —
launch coverage decays as products are superseded — so the buying guides and comparisons carry
the compounding load while launch reviews carry the spikes.

**The refresh is the highest-ROI action in this archetype.** A "best [category] in [YEAR]" video
re-recorded annually inherits the previous version's search ranking. Retire the old one to a
playlist and end-screen it to the new.

## 3. Cadence & Length

| Setup | Long-form | Shorts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 2/week (8–10/month) | 3–5/week | Testing time, not editing time, is the constraint — plan acquisition weeks ahead |
| Small team (2–3) | 3/week (12+/month) | 5/week | Clears 12+/month: 8× view growth, 3× subscriber growth (`benchmarks.md` §4) |

**Optimal length:** 8–15 minutes for product reviews (`benchmarks.md` §3). Comparisons run to the
top of the band because each contender needs airtime; a single-product first-look can sit at the
bottom of it.

**Mid-roll:** 8:00 unlocks mid-rolls for roughly a 50% revenue increase (`benchmarks.md` §3) and
this archetype clears it honestly — testing takes time to show. Place the break before the
verdict, never after; a viewer who has heard the verdict leaves.

**Cadence must bend to the category calendar.** Schedule capacity around known launch windows and
around Q4, when CPMs run 30–60% higher than average (`benchmarks.md` §9). A buying guide published
in November is worth materially more than the identical video in January.

## 4. Title Patterns

1. `[PRODUCT] Review — [N] Months Later, Honestly`
2. `[PRODUCT A] vs [PRODUCT B]: Which Should You Actually Buy?`
3. `Best [CATEGORY] for [USE CASE] in [YEAR] — Tested`
4. `Don't Buy [PRODUCT] Until You Watch This`
5. `I Tested [N] [CATEGORY] — Only [SMALL NUMBER] Are Worth It`
6. `[PRODUCT]: What the Reviews Aren't Telling You`
7. `Is [PRODUCT] Worth $[PRICE]? [TIME PERIOD] Test`
8. `[PRODUCT] — [N] Problems Nobody Mentions`
9. `[CHEAPER PRODUCT] vs [EXPENSIVE PRODUCT]: Is the Difference Real?`
10. `The Best [CATEGORY] Under $[PRICE] ([YEAR] Update)`

**Length rule:** search-dominant archetype, so 60–70 characters is the working band — longer
titles carry more matchable terms and that finding is drawn from search traffic
(`benchmarks.md` §6). The product name (or both product names, in a comparison) must sit inside
the first 40–50 characters, because that is all mobile shows and the product name is what the
viewer is scanning for. Include the price or the year when either is the deciding variable;
numbers in titles lift CTR 20–30% (`benchmarks.md` §6). **Non-English channel:** the formulas above are English syntax and must be re-derived from the target-language audience's actual search phrasing — noting that product names and spec terms are frequently kept in English even inside non-English queries (`references/localization-guide.md` §5).

## 5. Thumbnail Formula

- **Face:** optional, and most useful for a verdict expression — skeptical, unimpressed,
  convinced. Faces lift CTR 20–30% (`benchmarks.md` §7), but never at the cost of the product
  being unrecognizable.
- **Text:** ≤5 words, 3 ideal (`benchmarks.md` §7). A price, a score, or a verdict word — `NOT WORTH IT`,
  `$40 CHEAPER`.
- **Colors:** 2–3 primaries with the product isolated on high contrast. Consistency across the
  channel matters here because returning buyers look for a source they already trust.
- **Composition:** one focal point (`benchmarks.md` §7) with 30–40% negative space. For
  comparisons, a clean split with both products at equal scale and a visual verdict marker
  outperforms a collage. The product must be identifiable at mobile size — 70%+ of views are
  mobile.
- **Avoid:** manufacturer press images (every competing thumbnail uses the same one), boxes and
  packaging, spec sheets, more than two products in frame, and red-circle-plus-shocked-face
  treatments — they read as untrustworthy in exactly the archetype where trust is the product.
- **Target CTR:** 7.5% is the Tech & Reviews niche average (`benchmarks.md` §1); 10%+ is
  exceptional. Diagnose individual videos against the traffic-source table, not the niche
  average.

## 6. Hook Style

**Primary — Negative Framing.** Lead with the reason not to buy. It is the fastest possible proof
that the review is not sponsored theatre, and it holds the viewer through the positive case that
follows.
> "Three things are wrong with this, and one of them should stop you buying it. The other two
> shouldn't."

**Secondary — Demonstration.** Show the product failing or succeeding at the exact task the buyer
cares about, before any talking.
> "Same test, both units, no editing between them. Watch the left one."

**Timing:** state the value proposition within 15 seconds (+18% retention at the 1-minute mark,
`benchmarks.md` §2) and give the shape of the verdict early — "worth it, with two caveats" —
rather than withholding it to the end. Withholding the verdict does not create suspense in a
purchase decision; it sends the viewer to a competitor who states theirs.

**Disclosure is part of the hook.** Where the unit was gifted, paid or affiliate-linked, disclose
verbally within the first 15–30 seconds and in the first two lines of the description
(`benchmarks.md` §9). YouTube's paid-promotion toggle is not sufficient on its own, penalties
run to $53,088 per violation, and both brand and creator are liable.

Full taxonomy in `references/hook-library.md`.

## 7. Monetization Stack

> All figures are **US baseline**. Apply `references/localization-guide.md` before quoting
> revenue for a non-US channel.

| Rank | Stream | Why This Position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shopping affiliate | Revenue attaches to the decision itself — 5–20% commission, median ~15%, 60 products per video, 30-day attribution (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 2 | Brand deals | Purchase-intent audiences command the highest rates; floor is $1,000+ and 50K–250K views pay $1,250–6,250 per video (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 3 | AdSense | Consumer-tech RPM is only $6–12 (`benchmarks.md` §9), but high commercial intent and Q4's 30–60% CPM lift make it a reliable third |
| 4 | External funnels | A deal newsletter or buying-guide site captures the intent AdSense leaves on the table and is not subject to platform risk |
| 5 | Memberships | Works only where testing is the product — early access to results, raw test data, a request queue |
| 6 | Super Chat / Thanks | Weak; the format is not live and the relationship is transactional by design |
| 7 | Shorts ad share | Reach and launch-day speed instrument. Long-form RPM runs 10–100× Shorts RPM (`benchmarks.md` §9) |

**Sponsorship rule specific to this archetype:** never accept payment on the product under
review. Sponsor slots belong to unrelated categories. The moment those two overlap, the channel's
rank-1 and rank-2 streams are both destroyed at once.

## 8. Growth Trajectory

| Tier | What Changes | Key Lever | Revenue Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–500 | Nothing is monetized yet, but the category chosen in this tier sets the archetype's entire revenue ceiling | 25+ comparisons and buying guides in one narrow category | None — not yet monetized |
| 500–1K | Expanded YPP at 500 subscribers unlocks Shopping (`benchmarks.md` §9), so affiliate revenue starts before ad revenue does — earlier than in any other archetype | Turn Shopping on the moment it unlocks and link every product reviewed | First real income, and it is affiliate rather than advertising |
| 1K–10K | Full YPP at 1,000 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9) layers ads on top of affiliate, and launch-window videos begin compounding | First launch-window hero video; affiliate volume compounds with catalog depth | Meaningful but not replacement income; affiliate typically still outweighs the Tier 2 RPM band (Consumer Tech / Reviews, `benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 10K–50K | Vendors start supplying review units and brand deals reach the floor (`benchmarks.md` §9), which cuts production cost as well as raising revenue | Vendor relationships and review units; sponsorships structured so they cannot compromise the reviews | Can support full-time focus, with income set by category price point as much as by audience size |
| 50K–100K+ | The channel is a category authority whose recommendation moves purchases, and the buying season carries the year | Category authority and a Q4 buying-guide slate — Q4 CPMs run above the annual average (`benchmarks.md` §9) | Replacement-income territory, driven by affiliate commission on category price point rather than by RPM |

This archetype monetizes earlier than any other because Shopping unlocks at Expanded YPP rather than at Full YPP, and its
upper-tier income is set by the price of what it reviews rather than by audience size — two channels of identical size in
different categories are not comparable businesses.

> ⚠️ Tier progression is not a timeline. `benchmarks.md` §11 lists growth-timeline and revenue-by-tier as known gaps — no verified data exists. Any revenue figure must be modeled from the creator's own audience size and geography via `references/localization-guide.md`, never read off a table.

## 9. Failure Modes

**The channel that never says no.** Every video ends in a recommendation. Views hold for a while,
then affiliate conversion falls even as views stay flat, because the audience has learned the
verdict before pressing play.
**Fix:** commit that at least 1 in 4 published videos ends in "don't buy this" or "buy the
cheaper one," and schedule the next such video before the next sponsored one goes out. Track the
ratio in the content calendar as a hard field, not a habit.

**Missing the launch window.** The review lands three weeks after release, into search volume
that has already collapsed onto whoever published in week one, and earns a fraction of what the
same work would have earned on day two.
**Fix:** maintain a rolling 90-day release calendar for the category and block production
capacity against each date now. For the next launch, publish a same-week first-impressions video
and follow it with the full test — the first captures the window, the second captures the search
term long-term.

**Single-product default.** The channel publishes one product per video because that is how
review units arrive, and never builds the comparison and buying-guide layer where the search
volume and the affiliate conversion actually live.
**Fix:** for every three single-product reviews already published, cut one comparison video from
the existing footage this month. Then add a standing "vs" video to the calendar every second
week.
