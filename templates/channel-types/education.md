# Education Channel

> Search-and-suggested driven. Viewers arrive with a question and leave with an understanding.

---

## 1. Channel DNA

Education channels are **compounding assets**. A single well-optimized explainer keeps earning
search impressions for years, which means the back catalog eventually outproduces new uploads —
at scale, most views come from videos published long ago. The algorithm rewards this archetype
for **completion and session continuation**: a viewer who understands something wants the next
thing, so playlists and end screens convert unusually well here. Revenue is mid-tier and
stable (RPM $8–15, see `benchmarks.md` §9), and the 8-minute mid-roll threshold is easy to
clear honestly because the format genuinely needs the runtime. The trap is that comprehension
feels like performance — a video can be clear, correct, and still fail, because clarity is not
a reason to click.

---

## 2. Content Mix

| Type | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Help** (searchable explainers) | 55–65% | The compounding base. Answers a question someone typed. |
| **Hub** (series, recurring formats) | 25–30% | Builds the habit and the subscriber relationship. |
| **Hero** (ambitious flagship) | 10–15% | Reach beyond the existing audience. 1–2 per year. |

**Evergreen vs trending:** 80%+ evergreen. Trending content is a poor fit — it competes with
news channels that move faster and it decays before search can find it.

**Updating beats publishing.** Refreshing a high-performing evergreen explainer is often worth
more than a new upload.

---

## 3. Cadence & Length

| Setup | Long-form | Shorts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 2/week (8–10/month) | 2–3/week | Sustainable while researching properly |
| Small team (2–3) | 3/week (12+/month) | 3–5/week | Clears the 12+/month growth threshold |

**Optimal length:** 8–15 minutes, deliberately below the 15–25 minute educational / video-essay
band in `benchmarks.md` §3 — that band describes the essay form, while this archetype's default
output is the explainer, which answers one question and stops. Run up into the 15–25 band only
when the video genuinely is an essay: a layered argument, or a subject that needs its own history
before the explanation can land. Padding is punished harder here than in any other archetype
because the audience came for efficiency.

**Mid-roll:** clear 8:00 to unlock mid-rolls (~50% revenue increase, `benchmarks.md` §3). For
this archetype that is usually natural, not a stretch.

## 4. Title Patterns

1. `How [SUBJECT] Actually Works`
2. `[SUBJECT] Explained in [N] Minutes`
3. `Why [COMMON BELIEF] Is Wrong`
4. `The [ADJECTIVE] Truth About [SUBJECT]`
5. `[SUBJECT] for Beginners — Start Here`
6. `What Nobody Tells You About [SUBJECT]`
7. `[CONCEPT A] vs [CONCEPT B]: The Real Difference`
8. `The History of [SUBJECT] in [N] Minutes`
9. `[NUMBER] Things You Got Wrong About [SUBJECT]`
10. `If You Understand This, You Understand [BIGGER SUBJECT]`

**Length rule:** search-dominant archetype, so titles can run 60–70 characters. The primary
keyword and the hook must both sit inside the first 40–50 characters (mobile truncation,
`benchmarks.md` §6). **Non-English channel:** the ten formulas are structural examples in English syntax — re-derive each one from the phrasings the target-language audience actually searches, and never translate them word for word (`references/localization-guide.md` §5).

---

## 5. Thumbnail Formula

- **Face:** optional. When used, a thinking or explaining expression — never shock.
- **Text:** ≤5 words, 3 ideal (`benchmarks.md` §7), ideally the concept name alone.
- **Colors:** 2–3, high contrast. A consistent palette across the channel builds recognition
  faster here than in any other archetype, because viewers return to a trusted explainer.
- **Composition:** one diagram, object or symbol as the focal point. Visual metaphors for
  abstract concepts outperform stock imagery.
- **Avoid:** dense diagrams, small text, screenshots of text, more than one idea.
- **Target CTR:** 4.5% baseline, 6%+ for a well-packaged video (`benchmarks.md` §1).

---

## 6. Hook Style

**Primary — Curiosity Gap.** Open on the thing the viewer does not know they don't know.
> "You've used this every day for years. Almost nobody can explain what it's actually doing."

**Secondary — Problem–Agitation.** Name the confusion the viewer arrived with, sharpen it,
then promise the resolution.
> "Every explanation of this starts in the wrong place. That's why it never sticks."

**Timing:** restate the promise as a concrete capability within the first 30 seconds. Retention
at 30s should hold 70%+ (`benchmarks.md` §2).

Full taxonomy in `references/hook-library.md`.

## 7. Monetization Stack

> All figures are **US baseline**. Apply `references/localization-guide.md` before quoting
> revenue for a non-US channel.

| Rank | Stream | Why This Position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AdSense | Mid-tier RPM but high volume and long tail; the back catalog earns indefinitely |
| 2 | External funnels | A course or book converts exceptionally well against a teaching relationship |
| 3 | Brand deals | Advertisers value the attentive, high-intent audience |
| 4 | Memberships | Works when the channel offers depth beyond the free tier |
| 5 | Shopping affiliate | Only when the subject involves recommendable tools |
| 6 | Super Chat / Thanks | Weak — the format is not live or parasocial |
| 7 | Shorts ad share | Reach instrument, not revenue (`benchmarks.md` §8) |

## 8. Growth Trajectory

| Tier | What Changes | Key Lever | Revenue Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–500 | Nothing compounds yet; the tier goes to building enough genuinely searchable coverage that ranking becomes possible at all | Publish 30+ explainers that each answer one real query, not one broad subject | None — not yet monetized |
| 500–1K | Expanded YPP at 500 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9) opens audience-direct streams, but the material change is that early videos begin surfacing in search | Playlists to lift session time, so one ranked video pulls the rest of the catalog up with it | Audience-direct only, and immaterial at this size |
| 1K–10K | Full YPP at 1,000 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9); the back catalog begins compounding — old uploads earn while new ones publish | Series formats, so a viewer who arrived for one answer has a reason to stay for six | First ad revenue at the Tier 2 RPM band (Education, `benchmarks.md` §9); modest, but unusually predictable |
| 10K–50K | Search authority makes the catalog the asset — output can drop without earnings collapsing with it | Complete-guide heroes, and first sponsorships at the brand-deal floor (`benchmarks.md` §9) | Meaningful but not replacement income; steadier than any personality-led archetype at the same size |
| 50K–100K+ | The catalog becomes an acquisition channel for something the creator sells, not merely an ad property | A product funnel built on top of the catalog; the catalog is the main asset | Replacement-income territory for this archetype's Tier 2 RPM band, usually only once a product sits on top of ads |

Education is slow to start and hard to stall. The least rewarding tier is the first one, and the compounding that makes
the archetype work does not begin until the catalog is deep enough to rank across a subject rather than a query.

> ⚠️ Tier progression is not a timeline. `benchmarks.md` §11 lists growth-timeline and revenue-by-tier as known gaps — no verified data exists. Any revenue figure must be modeled from the creator's own audience size and geography via `references/localization-guide.md`, never read off a table.

## 9. Failure Modes

**Clarity mistaken for packaging.** The video is genuinely excellent and nobody clicks. The
creator concludes the audience doesn't want depth, when the title and thumbnail simply gave
no reason to click.
**Fix:** spend 30% of production time on title and thumbnail. Write 10 title candidates before
recording, not after.

**Scope creep into a lecture.** Videos grow past 25 minutes because everything feels necessary.
Retention collapses in the middle and the algorithm stops recommending.
**Fix:** one question per video. Anything that does not answer *that* question becomes its own
video — which is more content, not less.

**No searchable base.** The channel publishes what the creator finds interesting rather than
what anyone is looking for, so search traffic never starts and there is nothing to compound.
**Fix:** before the next 10 videos, map 30 real search queries in the niche and commit at
least 6 of the 10 to answering one each.
