# Commentary Channel

> Browse and suggested. Something happened; the viewer wants someone they trust to tell them
> what it means.

---

## 1. Channel DNA

Commentary is the archetype where **the back catalog does not compound**, and every strategic
consequence follows from that one fact. A commentary video's shelf life is measured in days —
the event it explains stops being an open question, and the video stops being watched — so unlike
education or tutorial, where old uploads quietly carry the channel, here the treadmill never
stops and last month's success contributes almost nothing to this month's views. That makes
**speed-to-publish a genuine competitive advantage**: the first credible take on an unfolding
story captures suggested traffic at 9.5% CTR from every other video about the same event
(`benchmarks.md` §1), and the second-best video published two days later gets a fraction of it,
which is why production quality is worth trading for turnaround in a way no other archetype
would accept. The algorithm rewards it accordingly — commentary lives on browse and suggested,
where the video is served cold, so the entire discovery burden sits on packaging rather than on
keywords. Revenue is structurally weak: Entertainment / Commentary is a tier-3 niche at $2–5 RPM
(`benchmarks.md` §9), so the archetype needs roughly four times the views of an education channel
for the same AdSense income, and advertiser-friendliness is a live constraint rather than a
theoretical one when the subject matter is conflict. The characteristic trap is **drift** — the
channel chases whatever is loud this week, the takes stop cohering into a worldview, and the
audience that subscribed for a perspective discovers there is no perspective, only reaction.
The counterweight is deliberate: a maintained share of evergreen retrospectives is the only
thing in this archetype that keeps earning after the news cycle moves on.

## 2. Content Mix

| Type | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Hub** (rapid-response takes on live events) | 50–55% | The engine. Captures the suggested traffic around a story while it is open. |
| **Help** (evergreen explainers, retrospectives, "how we got here") | 25–30% | The only compounding layer this archetype has. Non-negotiable, not optional. |
| **Hero** (deep-dive investigation or definitive essay) | 15–20% | Reach beyond the existing audience and the strongest subscriber converter. |

**Evergreen vs trending:** 25–30% evergreen — deliberately maintained, and the number that
separates a channel from a treadmill. Everything about the format pushes this share toward zero;
it only exists if it is scheduled.

**Two clocks run at once.** Rapid-response videos are scheduled against the news cycle and cannot
be planned. Evergreen videos are batch-produced in advance and published into the gaps. A channel
running only the first clock has no floor when the news is slow.

## 3. Cadence & Length

| Setup | Long-form | Shorts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 3/week | 5–7/week | Keep 2–3 evergreen videos edited and banked for slow weeks |
| Small team (2–3) | 5/week (12+/month) | 7+/week | Clears 12+/month: 8× view growth, 3× subscriber growth (`benchmarks.md` §4) |

**Optimal length:** two distinct modes. Rapid-response pieces should clear 8:00 — the mid-roll
threshold — and stop; padding a fast take costs the speed advantage that justified it. Considered
essays run in the 15–25 minute video-essay band (`benchmarks.md` §3), where 20+ minute videos
account for 57% of total platform watch time.

**Mid-roll:** 8:00 unlocks mid-rolls for roughly a 50% revenue increase (`benchmarks.md` §3),
which matters more here than almost anywhere because base RPM is $2–5. Place breaks at argument
boundaries.

**Shorts:** highest-leverage use in the archetype. A 30-second take published within hours of a
story breaking reaches the event's audience before any long-form competitor is edited, and Shorts
rank on completion rather than clicks (`benchmarks.md` §8). Note that Shorts are deprioritized
after ~28–30 days — which is fine here, since the take is dead by then anyway.

## 4. Title Patterns

1. `What Actually Happened With [EVENT]`
2. `[SUBJECT]'s [ACTION] Is Worse Than You Think`
3. `Nobody Is Talking About [OVERLOOKED ANGLE OF EVENT]`
4. `The [EVENT] Situation, Explained Properly`
5. `Why [POPULAR TAKE ON EVENT] Is Wrong`
6. `[SUBJECT] Just [ACTION]. Here's What Comes Next.`
7. `We Need to Talk About [SUBJECT]`
8. `[EVENT]: What the Coverage Left Out`
9. `How [SITUATION] Got This Bad — A Timeline`
10. `[SUBJECT] Is Not [COMMON CHARACTERIZATION]. It's [YOUR CHARACTERIZATION].`

**Length rule:** browse-dominant archetype, so keep titles under 50 characters — short titles
read faster in a scrolling feed, which is the surface this archetype competes on
(`benchmarks.md` §6). The subject's name goes first; a viewer scanning the home feed is scanning
for the name, not for the argument. Evergreen retrospectives are the exception — those are
search-targeted and may run 60–70 characters. If the channel is not in English, treat the ten formulas as structures rather than as text: the subject's name still goes first, but the phrasing around it must come from how that audience actually searches and scans, never from a translation of the English (`references/localization-guide.md` §5).

## 5. Thumbnail Formula

- **Face:** high value, and usually the subject of the story rather than the creator. Faces lift
  CTR 20–30% (`benchmarks.md` §7). A recognizable face mid-expression is the fastest way to
  communicate which event the video is about.
- **Text:** ≤5 words, 3 ideal (`benchmarks.md` §7). The text should carry the tension the face cannot
  — `IT GOT WORSE`, `THIS IS ILLEGAL`.
- **Colors:** 2–3 primaries, high saturation. Browse thumbnails compete against everything on the
  platform, and the viewer decides in under 1 second (`benchmarks.md` §7).
- **Composition:** exactly one focal point, 30–40% negative space. Juxtaposition is the
  archetype's strongest device — two elements in tension, or a face against a document or
  headline.
- **Avoid:** dense text, screenshots of posts small enough to require reading, timelines,
  anything requiring prior knowledge of the story to parse. Also avoid crossing into
  advertiser-unfriendly imagery: this niche already sits at $2–5 RPM and limited-ads status
  removes the primary revenue stream entirely.
- **Target CTR:** 3.5% is the browse baseline and 9.5% suggested (`benchmarks.md` §1); the
  Entertainment niche blends to 6.0%. Diagnose against the traffic-source table, not the niche
  average — 4% on a browse-dominant video is healthy.

## 6. Hook Style

**Primary — Shock/Contradiction.** Open on the fact that inverts the assumption the viewer
arrived with. It is the fastest way to establish that the video contains something the headlines
did not.
> "Everyone reported the resignation. The resignation is not the story — the timing is, and the
> timing changes what it means."

**Secondary — Stakes Framing.** State what turns on the outcome for someone who thinks this
doesn't concern them.
> "This looks like an industry dispute. It decides whether anyone else can do this at all."

**Timing:** the first 5 seconds must name the event — a viewer served this cold from browse does
not yet know what it is about. State the value proposition within 15 seconds (+18% retention at
the 1-minute mark) and place a pattern interrupt inside the first 5 seconds (+23% retention),
both `benchmarks.md` §2. Interrupt roughly every 30 seconds thereafter; commentary is talking-head
heavy and drifts into a steady decline without visual change.

Full taxonomy in `references/hook-library.md`.

## 7. Monetization Stack

> All figures are **US baseline**. Apply `references/localization-guide.md` before quoting
> revenue for a non-US channel.

| Rank | Stream | Why This Position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AdSense | Volume compensates for the tier-3 $2–5 RPM (`benchmarks.md` §9); high output plus mid-rolls makes it the base, but it is a thin base |
| 2 | Memberships | The strongest fit of any archetype — the audience subscribes to a perspective, not a topic, and 1% conversion is meaningful (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 3 | External funnels | Patreon, a newsletter or a podcast feed insulate the channel from demonetization risk that this subject matter carries by default |
| 4 | Brand deals | Constrained rather than absent — sponsors are cautious about conflict-adjacent content, so the floor of $1,000+ is harder to clear than audience size suggests |
| 5 | Super Chat / Thanks | Real when the channel streams reactions or post-event discussion; $50–500/mo for mid-size channels (`benchmarks.md` §9) |
| 6 | Shorts ad share | Typical Shorts RPM is $0.01–0.07 (`benchmarks.md` §9) — but this is the archetype where Shorts volume is highest, so it is not zero |
| 7 | Shopping affiliate | Structurally weak. There is nothing to recommend; commentary audiences are not in a buying posture |

## 8. Growth Trajectory

| Tier | What Changes | Key Lever | Revenue Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–500 | The channel is still establishing which beat it owns; under 500 subscribers it receives active algorithmic promotion (`benchmarks.md` §4), so distribution is cheaper here than it will ever be again | High output on one beat, until the channel is the obvious place to hear about that subject | None — not yet monetized |
| 500–1K | Expanded YPP at 500 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9) opens memberships and Super Thanks — audience-direct revenue arrives before ad revenue does | Convert the loudest commenters into members; this audience pays for access to a voice, not for information | Audience-direct only, and immaterial at this size |
| 1K–10K | Full YPP at 1,000 subscribers (`benchmarks.md` §9) turns speed into money, but a publishing gap now costs 2–3 weeks of rebuilt momentum (`benchmarks.md` §4) with no catalog underneath to cushion it | Speed-to-publish on a story the channel owns, plus a thin evergreen layer that keeps earning between news cycles | First ad revenue, suppressed by the Tier 3 RPM band (Entertainment / Commentary, `benchmarks.md` §9) — audience-direct still carries more |
| 10K–50K | Sponsors become reachable at the brand-deal floor (`benchmarks.md` §9), though brand-safety caution constrains which advertisers will touch opinion content | Opinion pillars that outlive the news cycle, an evergreen layer that is actually built, and sponsors chosen for durability over rate | Meaningful but not replacement income, and highly variable from one period to the next |
| 50K–100K+ | Membership base and off-platform funnel outweigh ads, which is what makes the channel survivable through a slow news quarter | Membership tiers and an off-platform home the audience follows the person to | Can reach replacement income, but the Tier 3 RPM band means ads alone will never get there |

Commentary starts faster than any search-driven archetype and stays far less stable: a single well-timed video can
carry a whole tier and a quiet news cycle can flatten a quarter, so these tiers describe volatility rather than a ladder.

> ⚠️ Tier progression is not a timeline. `benchmarks.md` §11 lists growth-timeline and revenue-by-tier as known gaps — no verified data exists. Any revenue figure must be modeled from the creator's own audience size and geography via `references/localization-guide.md`, never read off a table.

## 9. Failure Modes

**Pure treadmill, zero evergreen.** 100% of output is rapid response. Views track the news cycle
exactly, a slow month is a revenue cliff, and after two years of work the channel has no asset —
just an archive nobody watches.
**Fix:** schedule one evergreen retrospective or explainer every second week as a fixed calendar
block, and batch-produce two at a time so a fast-breaking story never cannibalizes the slot.
Bank them edited and unpublished.

**Opinion drift.** The channel reacts to whatever is loudest rather than to what it has a
position on. Takes start contradicting each other, the audience cannot describe what the channel
believes, and subscriber-to-view ratio decays even as output rises.
**Fix:** write down three to five opinion pillars — the positions the channel actually holds —
and put them at the top of the ideation document. Reject any story that cannot be connected to
one of them, and reject it in writing so the pattern is visible.

**Speed traded for accuracy.** A correction has to be issued, or the take is built on a detail
that turns out to be false. In an archetype whose only asset is the audience's trust in the
creator's judgement, this costs more than the views the speed earned.
**Fix:** adopt a two-item pre-publish check — every load-bearing claim has a primary source
linked in the description, and any claim that does not is spoken as uncertainty on camera. If a
claim fails the check at 90% edited, publish without it rather than delaying.
